Condition guide

How items are graded

Every listing gives a condition grade and a written description of any wear. The grades follow the usual bookseller terms, so you can compare with confidence.

Grades

New
Unused, as issued.
Fine
Close to new, with no defects worth mentioning.
Near Fine
Almost Fine, with a small flaw or two, each one noted.
Very Good
Light signs of age or handling, with any flaws noted.
Good
An average used copy or piece, complete, with wear described.
Fair
Well used, with obvious wear, but complete and sound.
Poor
A reading copy or project piece. Flaws are described in full.

Book terms

First edition
The first appearance of a work. A first printing is the first run of that edition.
Foxing
Small brown spots on paper that come with age.
Sunned
Faded by light, most often on the spine.
Toned
Paper that has darkened evenly with age.
Hinges
Where the covers meet the text block. Firm hinges mean the binding is sound.
Ex-library
Formerly a library copy, usually with stamps or pockets, always noted.
Dust jacket
The paper wrapper on a hardcover. Listings say when one is missing.
Bookplate
A label of a previous owner inside the cover.

Antiques and vintage

Age shows, and that's part of the charm. Listings note chips, repairs, replaced parts, marks and fading. Clothing lists measurements taken flat, any stains or repairs, and the original label size where there is one.
